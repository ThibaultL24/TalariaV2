Victor Hugo was born in 1802 in Besançon. He moved to Paris as a child and published Notre-Dame de Paris in 1831 in Paris. He lived in Paris through the 1840s and was exiled after 1851. He lived in Jersey in 1852 and then resided at Hauteville House in 1856 on Guernsey. He returned to Paris in 1870 and died in 1885 in Paris.

According to legend, Victor Hugo watched Notre-Dame from the quays in 1830 in Paris and vowed to save the cathedral with a novel. The story goes that he wrote standing at a desk in 1862 in Guernsey until Les Misérables was finished. An anecdote claims he left Paris in 1851 disguised after the coup. It is said that islanders saw him swimming off Saint Peter Port in 1860. Legend has it that he kept a view of the sea from Hauteville House in 1856.

He visited Brussels in 1851 and later lived in Vianden. He published Les Misérables in 1862 in Paris after years of exile.
